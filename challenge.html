YMCA Code Academy Challenge

A coding showcase built for young creators.

Students will create an original coding project and submit it online in one of two grade-level divisions. Judging will take place remotely before the in-person showcase.

Division 01 Grades 1–5
Division 02 Grades 6–9
●

Registration runs from October 12–23, 2026. The in-person orientation at the YMCA of Paterson and the online coding competition both begin on October 26, 2026. All final projects must be submitted by November 23, 2026.

How the Challenge Works

Build over time, submit online, and share your work.

This is a project-building competition, not a timed coding test. Students will have several weeks to plan, create, test, and improve an original project.

01

Register

A parent or guardian must complete the official registration form between October 12 and October 23, 2026. The in-person orientation will take place on October 26 at the YMCA of Paterson.

Register for the Challenge
02

Build Your Project

The online Code Challenge begins October 26, 2026. Students can build, test, and improve their projects at home until the final submission deadline of November 23, 2026.

03

Submit Online

Students must submit their completed coding project through the official Project Submission Form by November 23, 2026. Submissions must include a working project link and the written project information requested in the form. No video submission is required.

Submit Your Final Project
04

Join the Showcase

The showcase will be held in person at the YMCA of Paterson. Attendance is optional and does not affect judging, eligibility, or the student's ability to win.

Project Access and Showcase

Build online, then choose whether to attend the in-person showcase.

Most project work will be completed at home. The in-person showcase is optional and takes place after judging has already been completed.

Y

Students who attend the showcase may reserve a YMCA computer station to display their project. A separate showcase computer reservation form will be provided if needed.

Twelve YMCA computer stations are expected to be available for students to display, test, or present their projects on showcase day. Advance reservations may be required.

PC

Students attending the showcase may also be able to bring their own laptop, depending on final showcase guidelines and available space.

Students attending in person may be able to bring their own laptop, depending on the final event and computer-lab rules.

↗

Build From Home

Most students will create and test their projects at home. Students who need computer access may contact YMCA Code Academy to ask whether YMCA computer time can be arranged.

!

YMCA computer access during the project-building period is not guaranteed. Optional project help sessions may be offered weekly or every other week, depending on YMCA availability. Registration, final project submission, and showcase computer reservations will use separate forms.

Competition Divisions

Two divisions. One shared challenge.

Students will compete with others in a similar grade range. Projects will be evaluated based on age-appropriate expectations.

01
Grades 1–5

Younger Creator Division

How Projects Will Be Reviewed

Strong projects combine ideas, code, and clear communication.

Projects will be judged remotely before the showcase using a 100-point scoring rubric. Students will be evaluated according to their grade division and the strength of their submitted project materials.

!

Students will be evaluated according to their division and grade level. A beginning coder is not expected to build the same type of project as an older or more experienced student.

01

Creativity and Originality - 25 Points

Does the project show imagination, personal choices, or a thoughtful approach to the idea?

02

Coding and Functionality - 35 Points

Does the project work correctly and use coding concepts appropriate for the student's division?

03

Design and User Experience - 20 Points

Is the project organized, understandable, and easy for another person to use?

04

Explanation and Demonstration - 20 Points

Can the student clearly explain the project's purpose, features, and development process through their written project submission?
