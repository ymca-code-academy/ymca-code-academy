YMCA Code Academy Challenge

A coding showcase built for young creators.

Students will create a Scratch or Python project and submit it in one of two grade-level divisions. Participants may join the showcase at the Paterson YMCA or participate remotely.

Division 01 Grades 1–5
Division 02 Grades 6–9
●

The project period is expected to begin around August 1, with the community showcase planned for late August. The exact dates will be announced after they are confirmed with the Paterson YMCA.

How the Challenge Works

Build over time, submit online, and share your work.

This is a project-building competition, not a timed coding test. Students will have several weeks to plan, create, test, and improve an original project.

01

Register

A parent or guardian will complete the official registration form and select whether the student plans to participate in person or remotely.

02

Build Your Project

The project period may begin around August 1, depending on the website launch and final event schedule. Students should normally plan to build and test their projects at home.

03

Submit Online

Students will submit a project link or file, a written description, and either a recorded demonstration or plans to present in person.

04

Join the Showcase

The showcase is planned for late August. Students may attend at the Paterson YMCA or participate remotely if they are unable to travel.

Ways to Participate

Join from the YMCA or complete the challenge remotely.

Both participation options will follow the same project requirements and judging expectations.

Y

Present on a YMCA Computer

Twelve YMCA computer stations are expected to be available for students to display, test, or present their projects on showcase day. Advance reservations may be required.

PC

Bring Your Own Computer

Students attending in person may be able to bring their own laptop, depending on the final event and computer-lab rules.

↗

Participate Remotely

Students who cannot travel to the Paterson YMCA may build at home, submit online, and provide a recorded project demonstration.

!

Students should not assume that the YMCA computer lab is available throughout the project-building period. Any additional project work sessions will be announced only if they are approved and scheduled by the Paterson YMCA. Registering for the challenge and reserving a showcase-day computer station will be separate steps.

Competition Divisions

Two divisions. One shared challenge.

Students will compete with others in a similar grade range. Projects will be evaluated based on age-appropriate expectations.

01
Grades 1–5

Younger Creator Division

Start with creativity and clear coding.

Students can use Scratch or another approved visual coding platform to create an animation, game, interactive story, quiz, or educational project.

  • ✓ An original idea or meaningful personal improvement to a guided project
  • ✓ Working events, actions, or interactive features
  • ✓ A short explanation of how the project works
  • ✓ A live or recorded demonstration of the project
Explore Scratch lessons →
02
Grades 6–9

Developing Programmer Division

Combine coding skills into a complete project.

Students can use Python, Scratch, or another approved platform to create a game, tool, quiz, simulation, story, or educational application.

  • ✓ An original project idea and clear purpose
  • ✓ Multiple connected coding concepts
  • ✓ Testing, problem-solving, and project improvement
  • ✓ A live or recorded explanation and demonstration

What Can You Build?

Start with one idea that matters to you.

These categories are examples, not limits. Students will be encouraged to create projects that reflect their own interests.

▶

Game

Create a playable challenge with goals, scoring, levels, obstacles, or decisions.

✦

Animation or Story

Use characters, scenes, movement, dialogue, and sound to tell an interactive story.

?

Quiz

Ask questions, check answers, track points, and teach the player something new.

⚙

Helpful Tool

Build a calculator, organizer, study tool, recommendation program, or useful utility.

◎

Educational Project

Explain a school subject, community issue, historical event, or important idea through code.

+

Original Idea

Create something different that does not fit neatly into one of the other categories.

How Projects Will Be Reviewed

Strong projects combine ideas, code, and clear communication.

Final judging rules and scoring values will be confirmed before registration opens. The categories below show what participants should focus on while building.

!

Students will be evaluated according to their division and grade level. A beginning coder is not expected to build the same type of project as an older or more experienced student.

01

Creativity and Originality

Does the project show imagination, personal choices, or a thoughtful approach to the idea?

02

Coding and Functionality

Does the project work correctly and use coding concepts appropriate for the student's division?

03

Design and User Experience

Is the project organized, understandable, and easy for another person to use?

04

Explanation and Demonstration

Can the student clearly explain the project's purpose, features, and development process through a live or recorded demonstration?
